Catalyzing Transformative STEM Learning

At the CATALYST Lab, we advance innovative and inclusive STEM education through interdisciplinary research on teaching, learning, and student development across educational settings. Our work explores how evidence-based pedagogy, experiential learning, computational simulations, AI, and emerging technologies can deepen conceptual understanding, strengthen STEM identity and motivation, and expand meaningful opportunities for students to engage and succeed in STEM.

Research Pillars

🔬

STEM TEACHING & LEARNING

Innovative & Evidence-Based STEM Pedagogy

Investigating evidence-based and innovative approaches to STEM teaching and learning that strengthen conceptual understanding, problem solving, engagement, and academic success across diverse educational settings.

💡

STEM IDENTITY & PARTICIPATION

Identity, Motivation & STEM Pathways

Examining how STEM learning experiences shape students’ identity, motivation, belonging, persistence, and pathways into and through STEM, with particular attention to expanding meaningful participation and opportunity.

🤖

INNOVATION & EMERGING TECHNOLOGIES

Simulation, AI & Immersive Learning

Exploring how computational simulations, modeling, artificial intelligence, AR/VR, and other emerging technologies can create engaging and transformative STEM learning and research experiences.

🌐

INCLUSION & ACCESS

Inclusive & Interdisciplinary STEM Education

Developing and studying inclusive, interdisciplinary approaches that broaden access to high-quality STEM learning and support diverse learners across K–12, postsecondary, and educator preparation contexts.

Principal Investigator

Dr. Lexi Hwang

Lab Director & Principal Investigator | Associate Professor, California State University, Los Angeles

Dr. Lexi Hwang is an Associate Professor at California State University, Los Angeles and the founder and director of the CATALYST Lab. She earned her Ph.D. in Special Education from The Pennsylvania State University, with interdisciplinary training in mathematics education, learning difficulties, and educational statistics. Her research focuses on innovative and inclusive STEM education, evidence-based instructional practices, and interdisciplinary approaches to improving STEM teaching and learning. Her current work also explores the integration of computational simulations, AI, and emerging technologies to enhance STEM learning experiences. As Principal Investigator of federally and institutionally funded projects, Dr. Hwang leads collaborative research and student training initiatives that bridge education, science, and engineering to advance STEM learning and broaden participation.

Project SPACE

Active (2023–2025; No-Cost Extension through 2027)

Simulation-Based Pedagogical Approach in Chemistry Education for All Students to Succeed in STEM (SPACE)

Project / Award Number: NSF Award #s 2247282 and 2247283

Funding Agency: National Science Foundation (NSF) HSI Improving Undergraduate STEM

The SPACE program is part of the project entitled, “Simulation-Based Pedagogical Approach in Chemistry Education for All Students to Succeed in STEM (SPACE)”, funded by National Science Foundation, award #s: 2247282 and 2247283.

The program will support the advancement of fundamental understanding of chemical reactions and its application in solving advanced science problems by using training in computational modeling and simulation skills for undergraduate students in engineering majors.

The program aims to tap into students’ funds of science identity as an asset-based pedagogical tool and incorporate evidence-based learning strategies not only to provide intensive learning support but to create a learning space where students can affirm and/or develop their science identity.
